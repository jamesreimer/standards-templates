# Review Integrity Template

Stable template ID: `review-integrity`

Template edition: `1.0`

Human-facing title:

> **Review Integrity Standard**

## Purpose

This template governs correspondence between a Review's claimed conclusion and the scope, reviewed state, evidence, method capability, limitations, and findings that support it. It prevents a result from appearing broader, cleaner, more certain, more authoritative, or more complete than the Review established.

It applies across reviews, assessments, audits, verifications, and inspections whose conclusions support later decisions, claims, or acts. It is usable by human reviewers and decision-makers with ordinary organizational records, without AI, agents, or automated review infrastructure.

## Source document

The reusable template is [`standard.md`](standard.md).

## Adoption

Use the repository-level [ADOPTION.md](../../ADOPTION.md) review before adopting this template into an organization-controlled standards source.

Adoption assigns authority within the organization's chosen scope. This repository remains provenance and a possible source of later improvements, not continuing authority over the adopted result. Publication of this template does not adopt or update a downstream standard.

The review-integrity rules are synthesized standards decisions. BCP 14 supplies the normative-keyword interpretation, not the review model.

## Subject-specific adoption review

Use the universal review in [ADOPTION.md](../../ADOPTION.md) first. For this subject, also determine:

- which existing review, audit, or assessment systems govern the intended scope and whether adoption would duplicate sufficient authority or conflict with their responsibilities;
- what terms such as “clean,” “accepted,” “ready,” “verified,” or “independent” currently mean and whether those meanings exceed the scope or evidence behind the conclusions;
- which subject-specific evidence, result, and reassessment requirements already govern intended Reviews, and how allocation by matter preserves them without adding a second requirement for the same matter;
- how reviewed states are distinguished and how existing identity or provenance mechanisms support later reliance;
- where Material Findings, their effects, and material limitations are retained, and whether existing records keep them visible for later reliance;
- who has authority for dispositions needed by later reliance, and whether current practice mistakes a recommendation or classification for an authorized disposition;
- whether review conclusions are conflated with execution, publication, or adoption authority;
- which independence requirements already apply and whether current independence claims rest on evidence beyond the producer's conclusions, summaries, or assertions;
- how existing formats and records can satisfy the requirements without duplicate reporting, and what validation would demonstrate truthful scope, bounded results, and continued applicability after change.

These questions assess adoption safety and overlap. They do not authorize remediation, change a subject-specific disposition requirement, or create a new review authority.

## Likely organization-specific review points

An adopting organization may need to adapt:

- terminology and review-purpose categories;
- domain-appropriate reviewed-state identities;
- retention locations and records practices;
- finding vocabulary and the connection to existing disposition records;
- independence mechanics where independently required;
- existing review formats and purpose-specific guidance.

These are adaptation choices. The template does not require a review board, issue tracker, report schema, severity taxonomy, or AI or agent system.

## Conceptual boundary

Review Integrity owns truthful evaluative claims. More specific applicable requirements govern their matters; satisfying those requirements satisfies the corresponding Review Integrity requirement for those matters. Review Integrity continues to govern unaddressed matters without weakening, reinterpreting, supplementing, or duplicating the specific requirements.

The template leaves architecture, execution authority, publication integrity, standards authoring, provenance, organizational adoption, and domain-specific substantive rules with their respective owners, as described in [the standard's boundaries](standard.md#16-boundaries-with-related-standards). A complete Review may return findings or an unresolved result without establishing work readiness or authorizing later action.

Purpose-specific guidance can define procedures for particular Reviews. Agent-oriented guidance may layer stricter or more detailed behavior above the human-usable baseline, but cannot redefine its governance or make agent mechanics a condition of conformance to this template.

These relationships do not require adoption of sibling templates or establish a universal review workflow.
