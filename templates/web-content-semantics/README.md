# Web Content and Semantics Template

Stable template ID: `web-content-semantics`

Human-facing title:

> **Web Content and Semantics Standard**

## Purpose

The reusable template is [standard.md](standard.md). It defines content-meaning, semantic-structure, audience-understanding, representation-integrity, evidence, and claim requirements for a declared web scope.

It is independently adoptable; no umbrella or other companion is required. Images, audio, video, downloads, and embedded resources remain in scope when they communicate content, but “Media” is not a separate subject in the title because accessibility alternatives, responsive presentation, and control behavior retain their own normative owners.

This README is informative adoption guidance for humans and AI agents, not a second normative layer. Adopting the template does not establish content conformance, accessibility conformance, legal compliance, editorial approval, or factual certification.

## Adoption

Use the universal review in [ADOPTION.md](../../ADOPTION.md) first. Organizational authority, existing protections, conflicts, destination, provenance, migration, protected effects, and later-source review are handled there, not replaced by this subject-specific guidance.

The default template does not impose a brand voice, universal reading level, house style, vocabulary, content-management system, metadata format, or editorial workflow. An adopter may define those matters through its own authority when justified.

## Subject-specific adoption review

In addition to the universal review, determine:

- Which existing content standards, style guides, design systems, editorial policies, localization rules, structured-data conventions, and platform specifications already govern the proposed scope?
- Would this template duplicate an existing HTML-conformance, accessibility, editorial, localization, media, or metadata requirement? Which artifact should own each material consequence?
- Can assessed pages, views, resources, states, audiences, languages, variants, revisions, and exclusions be identified without implying broader coverage?
- Which systems or records supply authoritative facts for time-sensitive, conditional, eligibility, price, availability, or status content, and what existing ownership must remain intact?
- Do current content models preserve intended headings, sections, lists, tables, links, language, direction, figures, captions, and other relationships when rendered through templates or a CMS?
- Could migration alter URLs, labels, translations, embedded resources, structured data, publishing behavior, or protected editorial content?
- Do existing claims distinguish content-semantic conformance from accessibility, platform, editorial, legal, and whole-suite conclusions?
- Does candidate wording remain truthful before and after publication, with historical or provisional statements clearly contextualized?

These are pre-adoption safety questions, not a prescribed approval ceremony.

## Likely organization-specific adaptation choices

Review whether there is a justified need to adapt:

- declared audiences, content types, languages, locales, and domain terminology;
- house style, voice, spelling, grammar, and terminology conventions;
- content-risk tiers and the depth of domain, translation, or user review;
- sampling methods for high-volume user-provided, syndicated, or generated content;
- rules for time-sensitive content, source-of-truth integration, and currency review;
- content-model, CMS, structured-data, and metadata conventions;
- translation, localization, alternate-representation, and equivalence practices;
- evidence representation and integration with existing editorial or quality systems;
- stronger requirements for regulated, safety-critical, contractual, transactional, or instructional content.

Customization is not required when the defaults fit. An adaptation should not attribute a local convention to the unchanged template or collapse separate accessibility and content results.

## Verification considerations

Automated tools can detect some syntax, markup, link, language, metadata, and structural conditions. They cannot independently determine every intended relationship, audience assumption, misleading omission, translation consequence, content equivalence, or material contradiction.

Human review should compare intended meaning with rendered content, source or document structure, links and destinations, content variants, referenced resources, language and direction behavior, and machine-readable representations. Representative-reader or specialist review may be appropriate when audience understanding, domain meaning, translation, or consequence cannot otherwise be established.

An AI-generated review is not an observed reader response or domain verification. A passing validator does not prove semantic correspondence. A numerical readability score does not prove understanding. Missing access or competence leaves the affected result undetermined rather than passing.

## Boundaries

This template owns content meaning and semantic correspondence. It does not own:

- WCAG targets, accessibility claims, or the complete accessibility evaluation model;
- visual typography, spacing, or hierarchy;
- responsive layout, cropping, or media sizing;
- control behavior, validation mechanics, feedback timing, or recovery paths;
- performance, compatibility, resilience, or a general evidence system;
- brand voice, editorial approval, moderation, records retention, or CMS workflow;
- SEO, analytics, marketing strategy, privacy, licensing, or legal policy.

The same observation may support more than one assessment when it genuinely addresses multiple requirements. Results remain separate: correct semantic content does not prove accessible exposure, and accessibility conformance does not prove content accuracy or equivalence.

## Primary sources

- [WHATWG HTML Living Standard](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/) — living platform specification for HTML semantics and authoring requirements; relevant facts must be rechecked when materially revising the template.
- [HTML sections and headings](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/sections.html), [links](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/links.html#links-created-by-a-and-area-elements), [tables](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/tables.html), [images](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/images.html), and [media](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/media.html) — platform-specific semantic sources.
- [W3C: Declaring language in HTML](https://www.w3.org/International/questions/qa-html-language-declarations) — informative language-declaration guidance.
- [W3C: Inline markup and bidirectional text](https://www.w3.org/International/articles/inline-bidi-markup/) — informative directionality guidance.
- [Web Accessibility Standard](../web-accessibility/standard.md) — independently adoptable sibling owning accessibility targets, evidence, and claims.
- [GOV.UK: Understand content design](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/plan-manage-content/understand-content-design/) and [USWDS design principles](https://designsystem.digital.gov/design-principles/) — comparative first-party guidance, not universal normative dependencies.
